# 3D stage: guide for agents

`player/src/stage/` is the 3D view. Its element is created once and moved into each card, so the canvas carries on while the text changes. On an exit reached while something is still apart, the stage shows how the bike is held. Without WebGL it shows a text box; if three.js can't start at all, `main.ts` falls back to `PlaceholderStage` (`ui/stage.ts`).

Read first: `docs/decisions.md`, section "3D view". Then only what you need: `library.md` (library parts, how to add one), `gaps.md` (what still looks generated). Commands for the parts library are in `tools/assets/CLAUDE.md`. `npm run manifest` writes `build/3d-manifest.json` from `registry.ts`; `npm run check` validates content against it. `npm run shots -- --only road` (after `npm run build`) walks the rear-shifting tutorial and writes one screenshot per card to `scratch/shots/slow/NN-<card>.png`, and prints per card how far the drawn parts sit from each edge of the 3D view (`ThreeStage.framing()`); `-- --card <words>` limits it to some cards and also lists the parts drawn. Judge framing from those numbers; look at 2 to 4 pictures only to confirm.

## Files

| File | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `registry.ts` | Pure data, and the source of 3D names. `NODES` lists every name content may use (tag label, name, aliases, one line on what the part does, and `model_fact`: the text fact(s) with the part's model, shown in the garage preview). `NODE_PARTS` maps a node that stands for several meshes. Also `VIEWS`, `CLIPS`, `MOTIONS`, `LABEL_SCENES` (state label -> how the bike is held, what is hidden), and `previewNode` / `modelOf` for the preview tag |
| `spec.ts` | Constants, `BikeSpec` and `bikeSpec(facts)`: which bike to draw. The rules are in the comment above it (category, `bike.handlebar`, gear type, speeds, e-bike, `bike.colour`, `geometry.*`). Its dimensions (axle positions `rx`, `huby`, tyre `rt`, bottom bracket `bby`, frame points) are per bike; `RX`, `HUBY`, `RT`, `BBY` are only the defaults. Also `cassetteFor`, `geometryOf` and the frame-point helpers (`stayPts`, `batteryLine`) and the mount maths for the library cockpit (`cockpitXf`, `xfPt`, `Xf`: library space to bike space for stem, bar and grips) |
| `bike.ts` | `BikeModel`: builds the meshes (frame, wheels, fork, stand, hook, name decal) and calls the builders below. `add(id, geometry, …)` registers a part under its node id, `anchors` are where tags attach, `turns` are the screws and the barrel adjuster with their axis. `setDer` moves the derailleur and lays the chain. Re-exports the names of `spec.ts`, `geo.ts` (`seg`, `disposeTree`), `derailleur.ts` and `drivetrain.ts` (`rAt`), so other files import from `bike.ts` |
| `geo.ts` | Geometry helpers: `tubeGeo`, `curveGeo`, `gearGeo` (pulleys), `slabGeo`, `hullShape`, `seg`, `plate`, `v3`, and the toothed parts: `toothOutline` (roller seats and flanks on the chain's pitch circle), `sprocketGeo` (carrier windows from 28 teeth, splined hole), `chainringGeo` (ring with 4 or 5 bolt tabs, one mesh centred on the axle) |
| `mounts.ts` | Generated by `npm run parts:mounts`: where the library parts mount, in library space (crank spider and arm ends, seat axis and collar, saddle clamp and rest point, stem steering axis and bar clamp, bar half width, grip ends, lever and shifter spots). Do not edit by hand |
| `pose.ts` | `poseVals` and `mixPose`: how the bike is held (stand, hung, lifted, flipped), from the measured rest points of saddle and grips. Pure data, no three.js scene |
| `library.ts` | The parts library loader: `loadLibrary`, `hasLibrary`, `libGeo(name)` (see "Parts library" below) |
| `drivetrain.ts` | Library crank and pedals (pedals hang level while the crank turns: `BikeModel.setCrank`), a generated 5-tab chainring on the crank's spider, sprockets (`buildCrankset`; the sprockets are one block, `cassetteG`, that turns with the chain, not with the wheel), the chain (`buildChain`: inner and outer plates, rollers and pins as two instanced meshes; the outer plates have pin holes, and both kinds carry vertex shade, `shadeLink`; `layoutCirc`, `layChain`), `nearestSprocket` and `rAt` |
| `derailleur.ts` | The rear derailleur from the library pieces (`rd_*`): `buildDerailleur` (frames per moving piece, pulleys with generated 11-tooth rings on the library hubs, the long cage, the hanger with the B screw's tab, M4 screws, barrel or plain cable stop), `moveDerailleur` (pose, B screw, cable ends), `spinPulleys`, and for views and the cockpit `fitFor`, `upperPulley`, `lowerPulley`, `derLayout`, `barrelTurn` |
| `rd-rig.ts` | Generated by `npm run parts:rig` (`tools/assets/measure/rd.ts`): the derailleur's axes, pins, pulleys, barrel, clamp and screw spots measured on the source model, in library space |
| `rd-kin.ts` | The derailleur's kinematics on the rig: `rdPose(bolt, {body, link, cage})` (the four-bar linkage solved exactly), `pulleySpin`, `LINK_SWING` |
| `rd-fit.ts` | The rig fitted to one bike (`RdFit`): hanger bolt, chain length (big-big plus two links), cage length (`cageStretch`), and the angles for a position and a gap: link for the pulley's plane, body for the gap (B screw), cage until the chain is taut. The rules are in its comments |
| `cockpit.ts` | `buildCockpit` (library stem, bar and grips for flat and riser bars; generated drop bar, hoods and levers; brake levers and shifter at the measured spots) and `buildShiftCable` |
| `chain.ts` | The chain path around chainring, sprocket and pulleys, as a polyline and as exact pieces (arcs and runs); between wheels in different planes the chain bends sideways gently instead of cutting across |
| `pins.ts` | The chain as a ring of pins one pitch apart (chord): `layPins` lays them along the path from a first pin on the chainring, so on every wheel they sit exactly on the pitch circle; the sprocket block's angle follows from them. `chordLength` is the taut length of the chain around the path. The length left over is spread evenly over the free run before the chainring (a few percent per link at most). No three.js |
| `views.ts` | Named camera poses. `whole_bike` is in world space; close-ups are in bike space, so they follow a lifted, hung or flipped bike |
| `anim.ts` | `PEDAL`, `CRANK_SPIN`, `wheelStep` (the drawing rule against strobing: the rear wheel's drawn turn is capped per picture; not physics). Named clips (`shift-to-smallest`, `limit-screw-align`, `b-gap-adjust`, `cable-tension-shift`, …) and the generic motions. A `rotate` on a screw or the barrel adjuster plays the matching clip in the given direction |
| `visibility.ts` | Which parts a card draws (`visTargets`, `shownParts`; pure, tested in `stage.test.ts`): with `show` only focus + show parts, every other part target 0; without, the old context rule with the rest faded to 0.1 |
| `three-stage.ts` | `ThreeStage`: scene, lights and the prototype's r128 look (colour management off, lights × π), the pose for each way of holding the bike (`poseVals`), focus fade, tags (stacked so they don't overlap), caption, explore mode, replay, solutions, and the garage preview (below) |

## Garage preview

`ThreeStage` has a preview mode for the garage: the whole bike drawn from one bike's facts, turning slowly; drag to turn it, pinch to zoom, tap a part for a tag with its generic name and, in small text, its model when the bike's facts know it.

```ts
stage.preview(facts, host, { name, onPick })   // shows the bike in host (an element with a size)
stage.endPreview()                             // optional: show() for a card also ends it
// onPick({ node, name, model }) on a tap on a part, onPick(null) on a tap beside the bike
stage.snapshot(facts, width, height, name)     // a still PNG (data URL) of any bike, or null
```

- There is one stage (one WebGL context). `preview` moves its element into `host`, like a card does; give `host` a width and height. Calling it again with another bike's facts redraws.
- For a list of bikes, use `snapshot`: the same camera, a transparent background, drawn without touching the live stage (a few milliseconds per bike; cache the result per bike and its facts).
- The tag shows `NODES[node].name` and `modelOf(node, facts)`: the values of the node's `model_fact` facts, joined. A tapped sprocket shows as the sprockets, the upper wheel as the derailleur (`previewNode`).
- `PlaceholderStage` (`ui/stage.ts`) has no preview; check `'preview' in stage` before calling it.
- Without WebGL the host shows a one-line note.

## Changing the 3D bike: recipe

1. Shape or proportions of a part: edit the builder in `bike.ts`, `drivetrain.ts`, `derailleur.ts` or `cockpit.ts`. Keep the part id; content refers to it.
2. A new part that content may name: add it to `NODES` (and `NODE_PARTS` if it groups meshes), build it with that id (`bike.ts` or the builder module of its area), then run `npm run manifest` and `npm run check`. `player/test/stage.test.ts` fails until every node is built and the manifest matches.
3. Renaming a node used by content: rename it in `content/` and `registry.ts` together, then run the manifest and the check.
4. Which bike the facts produce: `bikeSpec` in `spec.ts`. Write a new rule as a comment there and list it in `docs/open.md`.
5. Camera: `views.ts`. Holding poses: `LABEL_SCENES` plus `poseVals` in `three-stage.ts`. Look and lights: `three-stage.ts`.
6. Check it: `npm run build && npm run shots -- --only road`, look at 2 to 4 PNGs, then run the `verify` skill. `stage.test.ts` checks that the derailleur, chain and frame stay off the sprockets in every gear of every kind of bike, and that tyres clear the frame, also with extreme geometry.

## Learnt

- Visibility: a card with `show` draws only focus + show parts and every other part has target 0 (faded over the change, then `group.visible` is false), a card without `show` keeps the 0.1 fade; "show all" in explore shows everything. When the `frame` part is not drawn, stand, rope, hook and the stand clamp go too (the pose still applies). The rule lives in `visibility.ts`, not in `applyFocus`.
- `frame_dropout` and `rear_hub` are parts cut out of `frame` and `rear_wheel` (`SPLIT_PARTS`, `NODE_PARTS`), not nodes: use `partsOf('frame')` and `partsOf('rear_wheel')` whenever you need the whole. They share the parent's `center` and `ctx`. `curveGeoSplit` cuts a tube by index, so the vertices are those of `curveGeo`. `drivetrain` and `rear_end` are group nodes (`GROUP_NODES`): `previewNode` never maps a part to them.
- Framing: on a card with `show`, a view in `FIT_VIEWS` (`drive_side`) keeps its direction but is re-aimed and moved (`fitPose`, pure, every frame, so it follows the picture's size) to fit the corners of every drawn mesh, with the crank's circle. Close-ups are authored; do not add them to `FIT_VIEWS`.
- `npx tsx tools/stage-digest.ts` also writes `geo` per bike, a hash of every triangle per part that does not change when a part is split; compare that, not `steps`, after a split.
- Software WebGL (shots, e2e) draws few pictures a second and `tick` caps its step at 0.05 s, so a 1.5 s camera move takes several seconds there; `shots` waits until `framing()` stops changing. A fixed wait gives pictures taken mid-move.
- Put new stage rules in pure modules (`visibility.ts`, `fitPose` in `views.ts`) and test them in `stage.test.ts`: `ThreeStage` itself needs WebGL and has no unit tests.

The prototype in `legacy/Bikefix-garage-guide v2.html` is the visual reference. Keep its look.
