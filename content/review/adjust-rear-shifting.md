# Review: adjust the rear gears

Doubts about the mechanics of `adjust-rear-shifting`, the diagnosis `gears-shift-badly` and their inspections. Nothing below is verified. The content uses the cautious wording described in each item until a mechanic confirms or corrects it.

Each item names the step or inspection ids it affects, so a change can be traced to the walkthroughs in `build/walkthroughs/`.

## For a mechanic

| Id | Affects | Doubt | Wording in use until confirmed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-01 | `check-high-limit`, `check-low-limit`, `check-low-limit-by-hand`, `check-b-gap`, `check-cable-tension`, `check-cable-tension-riding` | Screw directions. Assumed: turning H clockwise (screwing it in) moves the derailleur towards the spokes at the smallest sprocket; turning L clockwise moves it away from the spokes at the largest sprocket; turning the B screw clockwise widens the gap. All screws are right-hand threads, seen at the screw head. The barrel adjuster is seen from the side where the cable goes in, so counterclockwise turns it out and adds tension, at the derailleur and at the shifter alike. True for all common derailleurs and shifters? | Clockwise / counterclockwise, a quarter turn at a time (owner decision 2026-11-20). The barrel adjuster texts say where to look from. |
| R-02 | `check-high-limit` | The high limit is usually set with the cable slack. A too-tight cable can hold the derailleur in on the smallest sprocket, so the user may turn the H screw when the cable is the cause. Should the step first screw the barrel adjuster fully in to slacken the cable? | Changed after review (2026-11-17): the text says "If it does not move, stop turning: the cable holds it, and a later step sets the cable." "Further out" was a block until 2026-11-22; H is now a plain check (R-21). |
| R-03 | `check-cable-tension`, `check-cable-tension-riding`, `find-barrel-adjuster` | (a) Turning the barrel adjuster out (counterclockwise) adds cable tension, whether it sits at the derailleur or at the shifter. (b) Many road derailleurs have no barrel adjuster at the derailleur, only an inline adjuster on the cable, and some bikes have none at all. | (a) assumed. (b) "where the gear cable enters the derailleur, or where it leaves the right shifter." A user who finds none ends at "not supported yet" (R-15). |
| R-04 | `check-b-gap` | The prototype said "about 5 to 6 mm". The right gap depends on the derailleur; some 12-speed derailleurs use a gauge and a different sprocket. Is a gap check without a number useful, or should it only catch touching and very wide gaps? | No number. The text states a small gap with no touching (Next means it); two problem answers: touching, very wide with slow shifting. Points to the manual and names the model when known. |
| R-05 | `check-low-limit`, `check-low-limit-by-hand` | Changed 2026-11-20 (owner): L is set at the largest sprocket by the owner's method, then the hand push towards the spokes double-checks it. Is the hand push still useful with the chain on the largest sprocket? Can it harm the chain? | Both steps are safety checks with a block. |
| R-06 | `hang-bike` | Hanging the bike by the front of the saddle: does the rear wheel lift enough, and is it stable while the pedals turn? | Allowed with a warning that the hook or rope must carry the bike. |
| R-07 | `turn-upside-down` | Bikes with hydraulic brakes: should they stay upside down only briefly? E-bikes: can the display or handlebar parts be damaged? | Only the heavy-bike warning. No hydraulic-brake warning, because the brake type is not asked in this flow. |
| R-08 | `check-all-gears` | The stop "chain falls off or touches the spokes" after the limit screws were checked. Could this still be a limit screw a beginner can fix (a block) rather than a bent part (a stop)? | Stop, with "Put the bike back on its wheels" and the jump to `release-bike`. |
| R-09 | `derailleur-alignment` | Seeing a bent derailleur or hanger from behind is approximate. Small bends are invisible; a leaning derailleur is always serious? | Two states, straight or leaning/twisted. Leaning leads to a shop with `do_not_ride`. |
| R-10 | `chain-condition` | Visual only. Chain wear cannot be judged by eye, and wear limits are safety values we do not invent. | No wear state. The shop text asks the shop to check wear. |
| R-11 | `shift-test-ride` | Labels map "pedalling gets lighter" to bigger sprockets (cable too loose) and "harder" to smaller sprockets (cable too tight). Correct for standard derailleurs. Rapid-rise (low-normal) derailleurs, which react the other way round, are out of scope (owner, 2026-11-20). | As described. |
| R-12 | `ride-to-smallest-sprocket`, `ride-to-largest-sprocket`, `check-cable-tension-riding` | Test rides with a misadjusted derailleur. Since 2026-11-20 the user rides into the largest sprocket before L is checked (owner's order). Is that safe enough with the warning to stop pedalling at once if the chain moves towards the spokes, or should the test-ride path keep a hand check before the first ride? | The ride step warns; L and the hand push follow the ride. Every riding step is forbidden while the bike is held. |
| R-13 | all shifting steps | Bikes with front gears: should the user pick a certain chainring before checking the limits and the B gap? `drive.front_count` exists in the fact list but is not used. | Not handled. Steps talk only about the rear sprockets. |
| R-15 | `barrel-adjuster-present`, `barrel-adjuster-search` | (a) Where to look for an inline adjuster: is "in the cable itself, often near the handlebar" right, or does it also sit on the down tube or at a frame cable stop? (b) Bikes without any adjuster. (c) A bike with an adjuster at both the derailleur and the shifter: does turning either one change the cable the same way, so the user may pick either? | Three answers (owner, 2026-11-25): at the derailleur, at the shifter, "I cannot find it"; the last shows "Look along the cable" with "It is on the cable" (goes on) and "I still cannot find it" ("not supported yet" and a shop). The text says "If there is one at both, pick either." |
| R-16 | `check-all-gears`, `check-all-gears-riding` | "Go back to the cable step" relies on the user going back by hand, because the app never jumps back. | Text tells the user to go back. |
| R-18 | `check-high-limit`, `check-low-limit` | The owner's method (2026-11-20): turn the screw until the upper toothed wheel is in line with the sprocket; if the chain rattles or jumps towards the next sprocket, turn it counterclockwise until the wheel is in line and the chain runs quietly. Please confirm the directions: H clockwise moves the wheel towards the spokes, L clockwise moves it away from them, so counterclockwise is the fix for rattling with both screws. | "Turn the screw counterclockwise a quarter turn, look and listen again. Repeat until the wheel is in line with the sprocket and the chain runs quietly." |
| R-17 | tool set `derailleur-screw-tool`, fact `drive.rear_derailleur_screws` | Limit and B screws: cross-head or hex, and which sizes? Do some derailleurs use Torx, or mix heads (hex limit screws, a different B screw)? | Two options without sizes, "Small cross-head screwdriver" and "Small hex keys". When the bike's data does not say, the tools screen shows both with "a cross in the screw head needs the screwdriver, a six-sided hole needs a hex key". |
| R-14 | `shift-test-ride` in `finish` | After adjusting in a stand, shifting under load on the road can differ. Is a "still not clean" result a shop case, or should it send the user back to the cable step? | Stop with a calm text: you can ride carefully; a shop can check the cable. |
| R-20 | `derailleur-alignment` (animation `hanger-bend`) | The caption says "A bent hanger tilts the derailleur". The animation bends the hanger at its root, about the bike's length axis, both towards the spokes and outwards, much more than a real bend (drawing data, so it reads on a phone). Is that how a bent hanger usually looks from behind? Is inwards (after a fall on the drive side) so much more common that the outward bend should go? A bend can also twist the hanger about another axis, which the animation does not show. | Both directions, exaggerated; the text and the states are unchanged. |
| R-21 | `check-high-limit`, `check-low-limit` | Answers are the exception (owner, 2026-11-22): only "Further out" and "Further in" remain. The old answer "in line, but the chain rattled or jumped" is folded into the matching position answer (H: further in; L: further out), both turned counterclockwise. Can a wheel that looks in line still rattle, so that this case needs its own answer? H is now a plain check: the owner rates only the L limit as safety relevant. | "The chain rattles or jumps to a bigger sprocket" (H, further in) and "The chain rattles or slips off the largest sprocket" (L, further out). |
| R-19 | job `adjust-rear-shifting`, `service` | How often should the rear shifting be checked or adjusted? Is a distance the right measure, or also time (cables stretch most in the first weeks of a new bike)? | Unverified placeholder: every 1500 km (owner decision 2026-11-20: placeholders until a mechanic gives a source). |
