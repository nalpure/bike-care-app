# Review: adjust the rear gears

Doubts about the mechanics of `adjust-rear-shifting`, the diagnosis `gears-shift-badly` and their inspections. Nothing below is verified. The content uses the cautious wording described in each item until a mechanic confirms or corrects it.

Each item names the step or inspection ids it affects, so a change can be traced to the walkthroughs in `build/walkthroughs/`.

## For a mechanic

| R-01 | `check-high-limit`, `check-low-limit`, `check-low-limit-by-hand`, `check-b-gap`, `check-cable-tension`, `check-cable-tension-riding` | Screw directions. Assumed: turning H clockwise (screwing it in) moves the derailleur towards the spokes at the smallest sprocket; turning L clockwise moves it away from the spokes at the largest sprocket; turning the B screw clockwise widens the gap. All screws are right-hand threads, seen at the screw head. The barrel adjuster is seen from the side where the cable goes in, so counterclockwise turns it out and adds tension, at the derailleur and at the shifter alike. True for all common derailleurs and shifters? | Clockwise / counterclockwise, a quarter turn at a time (owner decision 2026-11-20). The barrel adjuster texts say where to look from. |
| R-02 | `check-high-limit` | The high limit is usually set with the cable slack. A too-tight cable can hold the derailleur in on the smallest sprocket, so the user may turn the H screw when the cable is the cause. Should the step first screw the barrel adjuster fully in to slacken the cable? | Changed after review (2026-11-17): the text says "If it does not move, stop turning: the cable holds it, and a later step sets the cable." | R-03 | `check-cable-tension`, `check-cable-tension-riding`, `find-barrel-adjuster` | (a) Turning the barrel adjuster out (counterclockwise) adds cable tension, whether it sits at the derailleur or at the shifter. | R-04 | `check-b-gap` | The prototype said "about 5 to 6 mm". The right gap depends on the derailleur; some 12-speed derailleurs use a gauge and a different sprocket.
